Both are free and open source. Alcove needs no account, collects no telemetry, and works offline with local files.

Alcove

Version 0.2.9 Windows desktop organizer

Keep a project's documents in one drawer and your app shortcuts in another. Alcove opens one drawer at a time, so you can find what you need without looking through every icon on your desktop. Name and arrange drawers on each monitor. It matches your wallpaper and lets you search with Ctrl+Space. The files stay where they are, and opening one still uses its usual Windows app.

ForgeKit

A coding agent can say it's done while tests are missing or part of the feature is still unconnected. ForgeKit installs Forge, a workflow that tracks a change from the first discussion through implementation, testing and review. Checks at each stage help catch unfinished work. It supports seven coding agents.

02 How we work What we care about

How we build our tools.

We care about details like where a file goes when you delete it, how you get back to your usual desktop, and what still works without an internet connection.

Windows integration

Use what's already in Windows

Alcove opens files in their usual apps and sends deleted files to the Windows Recycle Bin. Your taskbar keeps working. Those familiar behaviours matter when you're changing something you use every day.

Scope

Keep the job manageable

Each tool has a specific job. For Alcove, that's organizing the desktop and finding what's on it. It's still early software, so we publish release notes and call out known limitations alongside the download.

Local files

Your files stay yours

Putting an icon in an Alcove drawer doesn't move the file. Your layout is saved locally, and organizing and opening local files works offline. Alcove has no account or telemetry. It connects to check for and download updates.
