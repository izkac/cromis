# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

A deliberately mixed audience, in priority order:

1. Windows power users who notice the difference between native and wrapped software, hate accounts and telemetry, and would install Alcove on their own machines.
2. Developers and technical peers who judge Cromis by its craft and may read the published source on GitHub.
3. Potential contract clients for desktop, systems, and tooling work (Rust, TypeScript, Delphi, awkward Windows integration).

Their context: they land on the page skeptical. They have seen a hundred tool pages overpromise. They decide within seconds whether this outfit is worth another minute.

## Product Purpose

The site is the public face of Cromis, a small software studio, and of Alcove, its Windows desktop organizer (in early development, build not yet shipping). The single most wanted outcome of a visit is **Get Alcove**: today that means the notify list ("tell me when it ships"), tomorrow the download. Secondary outcomes: GitHub source visits and contract inquiries.

Success looks like: a visitor believes Cromis ships what it describes, trusts the "no account, no telemetry, your files stay yours" claims because the page itself behaves that way, and leaves their email or a star.

## Brand Personality

Quiet craft, understated. Confident without raising its voice. The page should feel like the software: small, finished, fast, nothing wasted. Proof over promises, specifics over adjectives. Reference temperament: Teenage Engineering restraint; a well-made tool's manual, not a keynote.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: gradient blobs, hero-metric stats, identical icon-card grids, "supercharge your workflow" copy.
- Crypto/dev neon: neon-on-black, decorative glassmorphism, purple-glow clichés (the current site sits too close to this).
- Corporate enterprise: stock imagery, blue-and-grey, "solutions" language.
- Brutalist or noisy: deliberately raw, maximalist, hard to read.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** The page is the product demo: fast, no trackers, no dark patterns, nothing it doesn't need. If a claim appears in the copy, the page should embody it.
2. **Show, don't tell.** Real screenshots of the real UI carry the pitch. Adjectives are cheap; the shelf rail and the drawers are the argument.
3. **Finished, not featured.** Fewer sections, each complete. No filler blocks, no placeholder grids, nothing "coming soon" that isn't explicitly dated or honest about it.
4. **Quiet confidence.** Restraint in color, motion, and word count. Emphasis through precision, not volume.
5. **Every word earns its place.** Copy is specific and falsifiable ("reads your real Desktop", "works with the network unplugged"), never atmospheric.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA: verified contrast in both dark and light themes, visible focus states, semantic landmarks, reduced-motion support, keyboard-complete navigation. The existing dark/light theme toggle stays.
